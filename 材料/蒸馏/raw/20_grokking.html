Small Enough to Know Everything:
The Fully-Enumerable Transformer as an Instrument
for the Science of Delayed Generalization

Yoshiyuki Ootani
Independent Researcher
info@ootanl.com
Abstract

Tiny transformers trained on fully-enumerable tasks occupy an unusual position in the study of grokking: every input can be evaluated, every generalization ceiling can be computed exactly, and hundreds of seeds cost minutes. We argue that this regime is best understood not as a scaled-down imitation of large-model training but as a scientific instrument with four capabilities that approximate settings cannot offer: (a) theorem-grade generalization ceilings that turn “can this split be generalized?” into a computed, falsifiable bound; (b) invariant-preserving task surgery, which manipulates a single structural variable while provably fixing all others; (c) direct observation of every weight without probes; and (d) survival-time statistics over 30–300 seeds that recast “does not grok” as a censored observation rather than a failure to replicate. The obvious objection is that laws characterized at 10410^{4} parameters may not mean anything beyond them. We answer it with a preregistered conservation study: three task-side laws established at 12K parameters—an exact recoverability-ceiling law, a role-conflict delay law, and a weight-decay response law—are re-measured, under an identical from-scratch protocol at 12K, 1M, and 50M parameters (4,000×4{,}000\times span; 360 new runs plus a 44-run preregistered control arm). The ceiling law and the delay law are conserved (0/144 Holm-corrected ceiling violations; Spearman ρ≥0.75\rho\geq 0.75 between role-conflict distance and grokking onset at every scale, permutation p<10−4p<10^{-4}), while the weight-decay law deforms systematically: its response steepens with scale in a direction consistent with larger models tolerating stronger regularization. Conservation was not assumed but tested against criteria frozen before data collection, and one law’s deformation shows the test could have failed. Together these results license the fully-enumerable transformer as a model organism for the task-side laws of delayed generalization: what it measures exactly, larger models largely obey—and where they deviate, the deviation is itself lawful and measurable.

1 Introduction

Grokking—delayed generalization long after training accuracy saturates [1]—has become a standard lens on how neural networks form structured solutions. Most quantitative work in this literature already lives in a small, discrete world: modular arithmetic over ten digits, permutation groups, sparse parities. What is rarely made explicit is that this setting permits a mode of experimentation that is impossible anywhere else in deep learning. When the input space has a few hundred cells and the model has 10410^{4} parameters, nothing about the task or the model is hidden: the entire input–label table can be enumerated, the Bayes-optimal accuracy of any train/test split can be computed exactly, and an experiment with three hundred seeds finishes over lunch.

This paper makes two claims. The first is methodological: the fully-enumerable regime should be treated as a scientific instrument—a microscope for delayed generalization—with four capabilities that we state precisely in Section 2 and that degrade or vanish the moment enumeration is lost. Two of these capabilities (exact ceilings and task surgery) come from enumerability of the task; the other two (full-weight observation and cheap ensemble statistics) come from smallness of the model. The combination is what makes the instrument: exact predictions on one side, exact measurements on the other.

The second claim is empirical and answers the objection the first claim invites: does anything measured at 10410^{4} parameters constrain models three orders of magnitude larger? We formalize this as a conservation study (Section 3). Three laws characterized at 12K parameters [2]—(L1) an exact recoverability-ceiling law, (L2) a role-conflict delay law, and (L3) a non-monotone weight-decay response—are re-measured at 12K, 1M, and 50M parameters under a single identical protocol: same tasks, same splits, same tokenization, same optimizer, same budget, same multi-seed survival analysis, all from scratch (the 12K arm is a matched-protocol replication, not a reuse of the prior records). For each law we froze, before collecting any cross-scale data, a three-way verdict rule: conserved, deformed (the ordering survives but the quantitative form shifts systematically), or broken.

The results (Section 4) are: L1 conserved—the exact ceiling is never exceeded in 144 Holm-corrected tests and the ceiling-ordering of grok rates is monotone at every scale; L2 conserved—role-conflict distance predicts grokking onset with Spearman ρ≥0.75\rho\geq 0.75 at every scale; L3 deformed—the weight-decay response steepens with scale and its descending branch lies beyond the tested range throughout this from-scratch series. A 12K control arm run on GPU confirms that the CPU→\toGPU environment change does not move rates, isolating scale as the manipulated variable.

We emphasize what conservation buys. It does not claim that a 50M model is a big 12K model; mechanisms may differ, and our L2 scale map (larger models are less tolerant of role conflict, not more) shows the mapping is not identity. It claims something more useful: task-side quantities computed exactly in the enumerable regime remain predictive at scales where they can no longer be computed. That is the license under which a microscope earns its place in a lab.

2 The four capabilities of the fully-enumerable regime

Throughout, a task is a finite table T:X→YT\colon X\to Y with |X|∼102|X|\sim 10^{2}, presented to a decoder-only transformer through a tokenization EE, split into train/test cells SS, and trained with a fixed optimizer to a budget BB. “Fully enumerable” means every element of XX is evaluated at every checkpoint—there is no sampling in the measurement. The four capabilities, each with a one-line empirical demonstration from prior work [2], are:

(a) Theorem-grade ceilings (from enumerability).

For a declared hypothesis class, the version space consistent with any training split can be enumerated, so the expected accuracy of the Bayes-optimal predictor on the held-out cells—the recoverability ceiling, the best accuracy any predictor could achieve given what the split reveals—is a computed number, not an estimate. Ceilings turn “the model failed to generalize” into a decidable claim: either the split was information-theoretically closed, or the model underperformed an attainable bound. At 12K parameters, converged accuracy tracks the computed ceiling with r=0.95r=0.95 across graded task families, with zero violations in 36 capacity-varied runs.

(b) Invariant-preserving task surgery (from enumerability).

Because the task is a finite object in code, structural interventions can be proven to change exactly one variable. Two examples: intercalated-swap corruptions that provably preserve Latin-square structure while destroying an additive-potential decomposition; and the rolex construction used here, which sets the two operand codebooks to permutations πa\pi_{a} and πb=πa∘σ\pi_{b}=\pi_{a}\circ\sigma for a random (D+1)(D{+}1)-cycle σ\sigma, fixing both marginal distributions while setting the role-conflict distance Drole=dCayley​(πa,πb)D_{\mathrm{role}}=d_{\mathrm{Cayley}}(\pi_{a},\pi_{b})—how far the two operands’ codebooks are from agreeing—exactly. Under this surgery, DroleD_{\mathrm{role}} predicts grokking onset with ρ=0.77\rho=0.77 (n=60n{=}60).

(c) Full-weight observation (from smallness).

A 10410^{4}-parameter model can be read in its entirety—every attention map on every input, every neuron on the full input space—so hypotheses about internal structure are checked by exhaustion rather than by probe proxies. This is the regime in which complete mechanistic accounts of modular addition were first obtained [3, 4]; enumerability removes the last sampling step from such analyses.

(d) Cheap ensemble statistics (from smallness).

At minutes per run, grokking becomes a rate science: Kaplan–Meier survival curves over onset epochs, Wilson intervals over grok rates, and right-censoring for runs that do not grok within budget—“does not grok” is a censored observation, not an anomaly. This matters because individual trajectories are provably fragile: changing only the floating-point environment flips the grok/no-grok outcome of 1616–19%19\% of seeds while leaving ensemble rates statistically unchanged. Claims below the rate level do not replicate; the instrument is calibrated to the level at which they do.

None of these capabilities is new in isolation; Section 6 delineates what is. The point is their conjunction—and the fact that the conjunction is exclusive to this regime. Approximate any one ingredient (sample the input space, grow the model, run three seeds) and the corresponding capability degrades from theorem to estimate.

3 The conservation study

3.1 Question and stakes

The instrument is only interesting if its readings mean something outside it. We therefore test: do task-side laws characterized at 12K parameters survive, under an identical protocol, a 4,000×4{,}000\times scale increase? A positive answer licenses the enumerable regime as a model organism—playing for the laws of delayed generalization the role that muP-style transfer plays for hyperparameters [5]. A negative answer, under preregistered criteria, would itself be a finding: the breaking point of task-side prediction.

3.2 Protocol identity

Everything except model size is held fixed across scales: the task tables, the per-seed splits, the word-level digit tokenization, batch size 16, AdamW at learning rate 3×10−33\times 10^{-3}, weight decay 0.10.1 (except in L3, where weight decay is the manipulated variable), a budget of 15,00015{,}000 epochs, and a sustained grokking criterion (test accuracy ≥0.70\geq 0.70 on two consecutive full-space evaluations; the first such epoch is the onset). All runs are trained from scratch, eliminating pretraining as a confound. Model configurations follow a standard Llama-style stack at three sizes: 12K (hidden 16, 2 layers), 1M (hidden 128, 4 layers), and 50M (hidden 512, 12 layers). Each cell receives n=12n{=}12 seeds (L2: 3 task instances ×\times 4 seeds). The cross-scale series comprises 360 runs; per-seed JSON records, including full evaluation trajectories, are released with the paper.

The prior study’s 12K records were collected on CPU, while all arms of the conservation series (including its 12K arm) run on GPU. A control compares 12K GPU cells against the CPU records at matched budget. This matters because floating-point environment changes are known to flip individual seeds [2]; the control confirms they do not move rates (Section 4).

3.3 Three laws and frozen verdicts

L1 (recoverability ceiling).

Task family addpotcKK: modular addition with K∈{0,1,2,3}K\in\{0,1,2,3\} operand rows fully held out. Holding out KK rows makes their 10×K10\times K test cells provably unrecoverable, giving exact per-split ceilings 1.00,0.71,0.43,0.141.00,0.71,0.43,0.14 on the 30-cell test set. Frozen predictions: (i) no run exceeds its ceiling at any scale (the ceiling is an information-theoretic quantity, hence model-independent); (ii) grok rates order monotonically in the ceiling at every scale; (iii) attainment—how closely runs approach the ceiling—declines with capacity, extrapolating the decline observed across 6.5×6.5\times capacity at 12K. Verdicts: conserved == (i)∧\wedge(ii); deformed == (i) with a systematic distortion of (ii); broken == any Wilson-significant excess.

L2 (role-conflict delay).

Task family rolexDD with D∈{0,4,8}D\in\{0,4,8\}, three instances each: exact Cayley distance between operand codebooks, marginals fixed by construction. Frozen predictions: the ordering of onsets by DroleD_{\mathrm{role}} is conserved within each scale (Spearman ρ≥0.5\rho\geq 0.5, censored runs ranked at budget); the absolute delay may shift with scale, and estimating that shift—the scale map—is part of the study’s value. Verdicts: conserved == within-scale ρ≥0.5\rho\geq 0.5; deformed == ordering intact with systematic absolute shift; broken == ρ\rho confidence interval crossing zero.

L3 (weight-decay response).

Task add at weight decay ∈{0.01,0.1,0.3}\in\{0.01,0.1,0.3\}, a three-point coarse-graining of the inverted-U response established at 12K, where intermediate weight decay maximizes the grok rate. Frozen predictions: the inverted-U shape is conserved; its peak may shift. Verdicts: conserved == the middle point exceeds both ends with Wilson separation; deformed == the response becomes monotone over the tested range while rates stay positive; broken == zero rates everywhere or an inverted ordering.

All criteria, cell lists, and predictions were frozen in a design document committed (with hash) before any cross-scale run was launched.

4 Results

4.1 L1: conserved

The exact ceiling is never exceeded. Testing every run’s converged accuracy against its split’s ceiling with exact binomial tests (Holm-corrected across all 144 runs of the from-scratch 12K/1M/50M series) yields zero violations (Figure 2), extending the earlier capacity-sweep record of 0/36. (The homogeneous binomial null is conservative here: the true per-cell success probabilities are heterogeneous, and the matching Poisson-binomial has smaller upper-tail mass, so the test can only under-report excess—which strengthens a zero-violation finding.) The grok-rate ordering is monotone in the ceiling at every scale (Figure 1, left): for K=0→3K=0\to 3, 12/12→2/12→1/12→0/1212/12\to 2/12\to 1/12\to 0/12 at 12K, 12/12→3/12→0/12→0/1212/12\to 3/12\to 0/12\to 0/12 at 1M, and 12/12→2/12→0/12→0/1212/12\to 2/12\to 0/12\to 0/12 at 50M. Prediction (iii), by contrast, is not confirmed: at fixed K=1K{=}1 the grok rates (2/122/12, 3/123/12, 2/122/12) are statistically indistinguishable across scales, so the capacity-driven attainment decline extrapolated from the 12K capacity sweep does not appear in this series at n=12n{=}12 resolution. We report the miss as the preregistration requires; the conserved content of L1 is the exact bound and the ordering, and capacity neither buys anything against the bound nor measurably costs attainment beneath it here.

A methodological note worth one paragraph: if the excess test is run against the best accuracy over the trajectory rather than the converged accuracy, four spurious “violations” appear, all in the K=3K{=}3 cells where the test set is noisiest. The best-over-trajectory statistic takes a maximum over ∼\sim150 evaluations of a 30-cell test set, and its null distribution is not the single-look binomial; the sustained criterion and converged-accuracy testing exist precisely to remove this multiple-looks inflation. Small-test-set grokking claims based on peak accuracy should be read with this failure mode in mind—a concern formalized independently by the measurement-validity audit of Khanh [6].

4.2 L2: conserved, with an informative scale map

Within-scale Spearman correlation between DroleD_{\mathrm{role}} and onset (censored runs at budget rank) is ρ=0.75\rho=0.75 at 12K, ρ=0.85\rho=0.85 at 1M, and ρ=0.84\rho=0.84 at 50M (n=36n{=}36 each; permutation p<10−4p<10^{-4} at every scale, 10510^{5} label permutations—the asymptotic normal approximation is invalid under this degree of censoring-induced tying, so we do not use it). All three comfortably clear the frozen ρ≥0.5\rho\geq 0.5 criterion. (The earlier 12K measurement of ρ=0.77\rho=0.77 on a five-level DD-grid is not directly comparable to these three-level-grid values; the ρ=0.75\rho=0.75 above is the matched-protocol replication.)

The scale map is the second finding (Figure 1, middle). At D=0D{=}0, the Kaplan–Meier median onset grows with scale (12K: 700 epochs; 1M: 900; 50M: 2,300). Away from D=0D{=}0 the same direction appears as collapsing rates: at D=4D{=}4 the grok rate falls from 7/127/12 at 12K (KM median 14,20014{,}200, barely inside budget) to 2/122/12 at 1M and 0/120/12 at 50M; at D=8D{=}8, from 6/126/12 at 12K to 0/120/12 at both larger scales. Larger models are less, not more, tolerant of role conflict per epoch of training. Whatever intuition says scale should wash out a representational obstruction, the data say the obstruction’s cost grows.

Controls: neither learning rate nor budget explains the deficit.

The natural objection is that the fixed protocol (learning rate 3×10−33\times 10^{-3}, 15K epochs, chosen at 12K) is simply wrong for 50M, and the D=4D{=}4 failure is an optimization artifact rather than a scale effect. We preregistered a control arm (design and verdict criteria frozen before the runs): at 50M, rolex4 was re-run at learning rates 10−310^{-3} and 3×10−43\times 10^{-4} (the direction muP-style scaling would favor for a wider model; n=12n{=}12 each, with D=0D{=}0 sanity cells), and separately at a 3×3\times budget of 45,000 epochs (n=12n{=}12). At both alternative learning rates the D=0D{=}0 cells grok (4/44/4 and 4/44/4) and training accuracy saturates, yet D=4D{=}4 remains at 1/121/12 and 0/120/12—below the 12K rate of 7/127/12 everywhere. At the tripled budget, D=4D{=}4 is 0/120/12 (best accuracy ≤0.65\leq 0.65). Under the frozen criteria, the scale map stands: the 50M role-conflict deficit survives learning-rate adjustment and a 3×3\times budget extension, and is not an optimization-mismatch artifact within the tested range.

The decisive comparison here is the within-scale differential rather than the learning-rate sweep on its own. Relaxation accounts of grokking predict a delay that lengthens as the learning rate falls (a clock scaling as 1/(η​λ)1/(\eta\lambda) in the regime analyzed by Kim [7]), so failure at a smaller η\eta is partly expected and cannot by itself exclude a different optimal setting for 50M. What no fixed clock can produce is the gap we measure at identical optimizer, learning rate, weight decay, and budget: D=0D{=}0 groks in all seeds run (12/1212/12 at the base rate and 4/44/4 in each sanity cell) while D=4D{=}4 groks in at most one of twelve, at every learning rate tested. The obstruction is task-side, and it is the gap—not the absolute delay—that the scale map tracks.

4.3 L3: deformed

The frozen conservation criterion fails, and fails informatively (Figure 1, right). The left arm sharpens monotonically with scale: at wd=0.01\mathrm{wd}{=}0.01 the grok rate falls 10/12→8/12→1/1210/12\to 8/12\to 1/12 from 12K through 50M. But the descending right arm—reported at 12K under the prior study’s protocol [2]—does not appear by wd=0.3\mathrm{wd}{=}0.3 at any scale in this from-scratch series (12/1212/12 at both wd=0.1\mathrm{wd}{=}0.1 and 0.30.3 everywhere). Under the frozen rules the verdict is deformed at all three scales, including the matched-protocol 12K arm: the response stays non-negative and ordered, but the peak has widened or shifted right. The two components of the deformation should be kept apart: the right arm’s absence appears already at 12K and is therefore protocol-driven (from-scratch versus the prior study’s setting), whereas the left arm’s sharpening is the genuinely scale-lawful signal. The data are consistent with larger models tolerating stronger weight decay for delayed generalization within a fixed budget, though over a three-point grid with both upper cells saturated at 12/1212/12 they cannot distinguish a right-shifted peak from a true plateau—saturated cells at n=12n{=}12 carry no power for that comparison. Pinning the peak’s location and scaling requires a finer weight-decay grid, which we leave preregistered for the follow-up (Section 5).

The left arm has a natural mechanistic reading in terms of the late-time relaxation account of Kim [7], whose clock is inversely proportional to η​λ\eta\lambda in the weak-regularization regime: at λ=0.01\lambda{=}0.01 relaxation is an order of magnitude slower than at λ=0.1\lambda{=}0.1, so runs at the smallest weight decay are plausibly right-censored by the 15K-epoch budget rather than incapable of generalizing. Two caveats keep this interpretive rather than quantitative. The result is derived for full-batch heavy-ball dynamics on linear (or locally quadratic) models, whereas our runs use mini-batch AdamW on a transformer—a gap that account itself flags as able to change the clock—and it describes the late-time loss tail rather than the abrupt accuracy transition our sustained criterion measures. Nor does it explain the missing right arm: a clock monotone in λ\lambda supplies no mechanism for one, but the prior study observed a descending arm at 12K over this same grid, so that difference remains protocol-driven. What the clock does supply is a model-side factor orthogonal to the task-side quantities L1 and L2 measure; the two compose, and separating them is part of what the instrument is for.

4.4 Control: the environment does not move rates

Re-running 12K cells on GPU (matched budget) reproduces the CPU rates within overlapping Wilson intervals (addpotc0: 12/1212/12 GPU vs. 3/33/3 CPU; addpotc2: 1/121/12 vs. 0/30/3). With n=3n{=}3 on the CPU side this control is corroborative rather than independently decisive; the primary evidence that floating-point environments flip individual seeds but not rates is the prior fragility study [2], which this arm is consistent with. Together they isolate model scale as the manipulated variable in the arms above.

4.5 Verdict summary

5 What the instrument makes possible

Conservation converts the four capabilities from curiosities into leverage: quantities that can only be computed at 12K (exact ceilings, DroleD_{\mathrm{role}}, split identifiability) remain predictive at scales where computing them is impossible. The research program this enables is a predictive one: a task-side function Φ​(T,E,S,H)\Phi(T,E,S,H) that outputs, before training, the expected ceiling, the grok probability within budget, and a predicted onset band. We commit to one concrete, falsifiable step rather than a prospectus: a preregistration of Φ\Phi—its functional form, coefficients calibrated on the existing task atlas, and its predictions for five task families never used in its construction, including one cross-domain family (compositional binding in enumerable worlds [8])—will be published with cryptographic hashes of the frozen predictions before the confirmation runs are executed. The present paper’s conservation map fixes the scales at which those predictions are licensed to apply.

6 Related work

What we do not claim as new.

Exhaustive evaluation of small algorithmic tasks is the standard grokking setting from its inception [1, 9]. Complete mechanistic analyses of small models are established [3, 4, 10], grokking appears well beyond algorithmic data [11], and mechanistic accounts of the delay itself exist at the level of training dynamics [12] and, most recently, as an exactly solvable late-time relaxation whose clock scales as (1−β)/(η​λ)(1-\beta)/(\eta\lambda) [7]. The model-organism framing has a distinguished precedent in muP hyperparameter transfer [5]. Delayed-generalization curves have been traced in language models of 35M–130M parameters [13], establishing that the phenomenon exists at scale; our question is different—whether quantitative task-side laws transport—and to our knowledge no prior work has tested law conservation under a frozen protocol across three orders of magnitude. Closest to our L2 axis, Howe [14, 15] shows that structure-specific representational priors causally control the grokking delay from the model side; our role-conflict law is the task-side counterpart (a structural distance computed before training), and the two are complementary rather than competing—a point the preregistered Φ\Phi program (Section 5) is designed to make quantitative.

What is new here.

(1) The explicit statement of the enumerable regime’s four capabilities as an instrument, with the enumerability/smallness attribution; (2) the treatment of generalization ceilings as computed, falsifiable bounds tested by exact statistics rather than as informal observations [cf. 16]; (3) systematic invariant-preserving task surgery as a design pattern; (4) grokking as rate science—survival curves with right-censoring over tens to hundreds of seeds, which goes beyond the 1–5 seeds per configuration typical of the literature and beyond generic multi-seed reporting; and (5) the conservation map itself—the preregistered cross-scale test that licenses (1)–(4) beyond the regime that generated them.

7 Limitations

The instrument’s writ runs exactly as far as enumeration does. Tasks whose input spaces cannot be enumerated—natural language above toy scale, vision—are outside it; the instrument’s role there is upstream, supplying calibrated laws and preregistered predictors, not direct measurement. Our scale series, while spanning 4,000×4{,}000\times in parameters, uses one architecture family and one optimizer, with one learning rate and one budget in the main arms (the control arm varies both, for one task family); L3’s deformation shows that budget interacts with scale. The 50M arm is a single hardware environment, though the 12K control and prior fragility results bound the plausible environment effect at the rate level. Finally, conservation of task-side laws does not imply conservation of mechanism; the L2 scale map (delay grows with scale) is a reminder that the mapping across scales is lawful but not trivial.
